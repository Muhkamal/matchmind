How it works

Three steps, all automatic.

Nobody (including us) touches a pick after it is written.

01

Lock

The nightly job fits the model, writes each probability to the ledger, and the database refuses anything created at or after kickoff.

02

Grade

When the final score arrives, picks are graded with proper scoring (Brier), not cherry-picked wins.

03

Show everything

The dashboard shows the record, the calibration chart, and the biggest misses. You see the failures first.

Guarantees

Enforced by the database, not by promises.

An append-only ledger

Triggers reject changes to the ledger tables. A unique index keeps only the first live pick per match, and a check refuses a pick created after kickoff.

-- what the database says
UPDATE predictions …  ✗ rejected
DELETE FROM predictions  ✗ rejected
second pick, same match  ✗ ignored
pick after kickoff  ✗ rejected
new pick before kickoff  ✓ appended

Calibration on display

If we say 60%, it should happen about 60% of the time. The chart is public.

The arena

Beat the model head-to-head on Telegram. Picks are final and close at kickoff. You appear on the board after 10 settled picks, under a nickname you choose.

Goal markets, held to a baselineexperimental

Over 2.5 goals and both-teams-to-score come from the same scoreline table as the match pick, so they can never contradict it. They are graded against "just predict the average", and if they do not beat that, the dashboard says so.

Honest limits

What we do, and what we will not do.

We do

  • Publish every pick before kickoff and every result after.
  • Show the sample size next to every rate and a baseline next to every score.
  • Explain the method: an Elo and Dixon-Coles ensemble on match results.

We do not

  • Sell picks, "slips" or booking codes, or promise profit.
  • Claim to beat bookmaker prices: the model sees results only, with no lineups, injuries or odds.
  • Hide the losses. They sit on the dashboard under "biggest misses".
